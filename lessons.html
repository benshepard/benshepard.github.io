Flight & ground

Private Pilot Training

A progression from the first preflight through independent decision-making, solo readiness, cross-country flying, and practical test preparation.

Instruction overview ↗
  1. 01

    Getting oriented

    Training goals, aircraft familiarization, required documents, preflight inspection, checklist use, and basic risk management.

  2. 02

    Aircraft control

    Straight-and-level, climbs, descents, turns, trim, coordinated flight, slow flight, stalls, and traffic pattern operations.

  3. 03

    Takeoffs, landings & emergencies

    Normal and crosswind operations, go-arounds, emergency procedures, and practical judgment in changing conditions.

  4. 04

    Weather, airspace & decision-making

    Weather interpretation, airport operations, airspace rules, performance calculations, and go/no-go scenarios.

  5. 05

    Pre-solo preparation

    Consolidation of maneuvers, emergency response, local procedures, and the applicable knowledge and proficiency requirements for solo flight.

  6. 06

    Navigation & cross-country

    Pilotage, dead reckoning, flight planning, navigation tools, diversion planning, and solo cross-country preparation as appropriate.

  7. 07

    Practical test readiness

    ACS-based proficiency review, scenario-based oral preparation, mock flight evaluation, and a final plan for any remaining gaps.

Training sequence and solo milestones depend on demonstrated proficiency and all applicable regulatory requirements.

Flight & ground

Commercial Pilot Preparation

Advanced aircraft handling and decision-making, with deliberate practice tied to the applicable Commercial Pilot Airman Certification Standards.

Instruction overview ↗
  1. 01

    Baseline & training plan

    Review current flight experience, certificate requirements, aircraft performance, and priority areas from an initial skills assessment.

  2. 02

    Precision & energy management

    Refine coordinated control, altitude and airspeed discipline, performance planning, and the management of energy throughout each maneuver.

  3. 03

    Commercial maneuvers

    Practice applicable chandelles, lazy eights, steep spirals, eights on pylons, and accuracy landings with attention to why each technique works.

  4. 04

    Operational judgment

    Work through weather, fuel, terrain, passenger, and time-pressure scenarios using professional-level risk management.

  5. 05

    Cross-country & aircraft operations

    Plan realistic flights, evaluate airplane limitations, and address complex or technically advanced airplane requirements when applicable.

  6. 06

    Practical test preparation

    Use the applicable ACS to identify gaps, practice oral scenarios, and evaluate maneuver consistency before the checkride.

The training plan is customized to the certificate sought, aircraft used, experience requirements, and applicable ACS.

Ground & flight

Flight Reviews & Proficiency

A targeted, practical review built around the flying you do and the skills you want to strengthen.

Instruction overview ↗
  1. 01

    Pilot profile & goals

    Discuss recent experience, typical missions, areas of concern, aircraft familiarity, and what would make the review useful.

  2. 02

    Ground review

    Address applicable regulations, airspace, weather, flight planning, aircraft performance, and risk management with real-world scenarios.

  3. 03

    Flight proficiency

    Practice relevant maneuvers, takeoffs and landings, emergency procedures, and judgment in the operating environment you use.

  4. 04

    Debrief & next steps

    Identify strengths and gaps, agree on a proficiency plan, and complete a flight review endorsement only if all applicable requirements are met.

An FAA flight review includes at least one hour of ground training and one hour of flight training, and may require more time depending on proficiency and circumstances.

Mountain Flying & Mountain Checkouts

Ground and flight preparation for operating near mountainous terrain, tailored to the aircraft, route, location, and pilot's experience.

Instruction overview ↗
  1. 01

    Aircraft performance at altitude

    Calculate density altitude, takeoff and climb performance, loading limits, and suitable margins for high-elevation airports.

  2. 02

    Terrain, route & escape planning

    Study mountain passes, route selection, terrain clearance, turning room, and practical options when a planned route becomes unsuitable.

  3. 03

    Mountain weather

    Evaluate winds aloft, turbulence, mountain waves, downdrafts, visibility, and weather-related reasons to delay, divert, or turn back.

  4. 04

    Mountain airport operations

    Discuss airport reconnaissance, approach and departure considerations, go-around limitations, and operational decision points.

  5. 05

    Scenario-based mountain checkout

    Apply preflight planning and, when appropriate, supervised flight exercises to assess readiness for the intended aircraft and operating area.

A mountain checkout is not a standardized FAA endorsement. Scope, any flight portion, and completion criteria are tailored to the aircraft, local conditions, and applicable requirements.

Ground instruction

Instrument Ground Training

A scenario-based ground curriculum connecting IFR knowledge to flight planning, procedures, and sound instrument decision-making.

Instruction overview ↗
  1. 01

    IFR framework

    Review regulations, aircraft and pilot eligibility, clearances, IFR flight plans, and the structure of the national airspace system.

  2. 02

    Charts & navigation

    Interpret IFR enroute charts, approach plates, navigation systems, minimum altitudes, and relevant limitations.

  3. 03

    Weather for IFR

    Assess forecasts, observations, icing, convection, ceilings, visibility, and the changing risk of a proposed route.

  4. 04

    Flight planning & alternatives

    Work through alternate requirements, fuel planning, route selection, lost communications, and diversion options.

  5. 05

    Procedures & briefings

    Review departures, arrivals, holds, instrument approaches, missed approaches, and concise procedure briefings.

  6. 06

    End-to-end scenarios

    Plan and talk through realistic IFR flights, combining procedural understanding with workload and risk management.

This outline describes ground instruction only; instrument flight instruction and any associated endorsement privileges require separate confirmation.

Tailwheel Transition

A progressive return to precise stick-and-rudder flying, from ground handling to consistent takeoff and landing control.

Instruction overview ↗
  1. 01

    Tailwheel fundamentals

    Review aircraft differences, landing gear geometry, directional stability, ground-loop risk, and applicable operating limitations.

  2. 02

    Ground handling & taxi

    Practice correct control positioning, brake discipline, crosswind taxi technique, and maintaining awareness of the airplane's direction.

  3. 03

    Takeoffs & landings

    Develop proficiency in normal and crosswind takeoffs, three-point landings, and wheel landings as appropriate to the airplane.

  4. 04

    Unusual situations

    Practice go-arounds, rejected takeoffs when appropriate, wind changes, and corrections for loss of directional control.

  5. 05

    Proficiency & endorsement readiness

    Evaluate consistent safe operation, discuss continued practice, and consider an endorsement only after all FAA requirements are satisfied.

Tailwheel transition instruction is a planned offering, subject to instructor eligibility, aircraft access, insurance, and training arrangements.
